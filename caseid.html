Symbotic builds end-to-end warehouse automation for some of the largest retailers and wholesalers in the world. Robotic cells break inbound pallets down into individual cases, a fleet of autonomous mobile robots stores and retrieves those cases in a dense storage structure, and outbound cells build mixed pallets ready for the store.

Because the system handles goods as individual cases in their native packaging, everything downstream depends on knowing exactly what each case is. CaseID is the system I led for that problem: identifying the SKU of a case from images taken by cameras on the mobile robots.

The range of products is effectively unbounded: packaging varies widely, new SKUs arrive constantly, and many cases look alike. As tech lead I took CaseID from ideation and literature review, through model development and experimentation, to production deployment and monitoring.

01

Metric Learning on a Foundation Model

The encoder starts from a pretrained Vision Transformer foundation model and is fine-tuned on case images with metric learning: the loss pulls embeddings of the same SKU together and pushes different SKUs apart. Distance in the resulting space measures how likely two images are the same product.

02

Search and Verification

The embedding is used in two ways. In search, it is compared against a gallery of reference embeddings and the nearest SKUs are returned. In verification, the system already expects a particular SKU, and the embedding is checked against that SKU to accept the case or flag a mismatch.

03

The Full Pipeline on the Robot

Detection and cropping of the case, embedding, and matching all run onboard the robot on an NVIDIA Jetson, as ROS nodes. Models are fine-tuned in the cloud on Azure, exported to ONNX, and containerized. Memory on the robot is limited and shared, so memory management matters as much as model accuracy.
