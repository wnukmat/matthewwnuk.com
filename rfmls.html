Radio Frequency Machine Learning Systems (RFMLS) was a DARPA program early in the deep learning revolution (2017 to 2019). Its goal was to take the lessons learned from convolutional methods in vision and apply them to the RF domain.

We designed, aggregated, and processed the largest machine-learning-quality RF dataset available, defined a set of challenging RF problems that the dataset was suited to, and evaluated participants' methods. It was effectively the government's RF-domain version of ImageNet.

The dataset consisted of complex-valued signals, which let us explore data preprocessing methods, complex-valued neural network architectures, and FFT-matrix layer initialization techniques.

01

Hardware Fingerprinting

By exploiting the perturbations in received signals caused by imperfections in the transmitting chips, we were able to tell apart up to 10,000 unique Wi-Fi transmitters.

02

Broadband SOI Identification with Confusers

We injected signals of interest (SOI) into broadband background recordings. Our models separated the SOI from the background and classified it correctly. Injections were made at frequencies and bandwidths that are not characteristic of the injected signal, which forced the model to learn non-trivial features.

03

RL Resource Management of Receiver

A bespoke broadband receiver was built for this project. It let us develop a reinforcement learning policy controller that exploits the spectrum to search for, identify, track, and maintain signals of interest while continuing to explore the search space.
